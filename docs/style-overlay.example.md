## RS / CBO corpus voice notes

The source documents in `~/RS-claude/` and the seed Mahalath corpus are
the operator's Coherent Biblical Ontology / Relational Substrate book
series. Definitions written against this corpus should follow the
author's framing, not the standard philosophy-dictionary defaults.

**Voice and stance**

- Definitions are *covenant-neutral but scripture-authoritative*: treat
  the book's biblical framing as load-bearing without adopting any
  particular denominational stance.
- Prefer **declarative ontology** ("X is Y") over interpretive framing
  ("X can be seen as Y", "X is sometimes understood as Y"). The book is
  not hedging; definitions should not either.
- Avoid metaphor when a direct statement is available. When metaphor is
  used, it should be bounded and clearly marked as such.

**Term overrides**

- **"Ontology"** in this corpus means *"what something really is"* (the
  book's opening framing), not the academic discipline of metaphysics.
  Definitions should preserve this stance.
- **"Substrate"** is the generic underlying medium that constitutes
  observable phenomena.
- **"Relational Substrate"** is the *specific* substrate the corpus
  proposes — fundamentally relational rather than particulate. RS is a
  kind of substrate, not a synonym for substrate.
- **"Vortons"** are stable topological knots within the substrate's
  weave; treat as domain-technical, not metaphor.
- **"Runtime physical order"** vs **"runtime order"**: the former is
  the concrete instantiation of physical reality; the latter is the
  ordering principle governing it.
- **"T0"** is a corpus-specific marker for the foundational ontological
  starting point; not the philosophical "zero point".

**Hierarchy guidance**

- More-specific concepts are children of more-general concepts. When
  in doubt about direction, the parent is the term whose definition
  could meaningfully replace the child's in a general context but not
  vice versa.
- **Relational Substrate** is a child of **substrate**, not the other
  way around. Substrate is the more general concept; Relational
  Substrate is its specific instance.
